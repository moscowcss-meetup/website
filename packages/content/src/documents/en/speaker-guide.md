## No experience needed

You do not need to have spoken before: many Moscow CSS speakers gave their first talk with us. The organisers will help with the topic, the structure and a rehearsal.

## Who we are looking for

Above all, frontend developers, markup developers and interface designers. Managers and developer advocates are welcome too, if they have something to tell developers.

## What to talk about

We are about development after all, so technical talks come first: CSS and HTML, browsers, performance, accessibility, tools, design systems. Management and soft skills matter too, but we have plenty of those in reserve, so a meetup usually takes one. The audience asks for more technical talks, and we hear them.

Example topics are at the end of the page. They are a guide, not a fence: if your idea looks like none of them, come with it anyway.

## Time

A talk takes up to 20 minutes, plus 10 minutes for questions from the audience.

## Slides

- A title slide: the talk's title, your name, your position or a few words about yourself. Humour is welcome: what you love and what you do not.
- A number on every slide except the title one: the audience refers to it in questions.
- Code on slides is short, typed as text rather than pasted as an image, in a monospace font and with highlighting that reads on the slide's background.
- If you prepared from articles and repositories, add a slide with links.
- Links are short and readable. Best of all, one link to a page with all the talk's materials: a short address or a QR code.
- The last slide says thank you and shows your name and public contacts.

## What to send the organisers

For the announcement:

- your first and last name, company and position;
- a photo;
- the talk's title, description and key points;
- public contacts, if you want them in the announcement: Telegram, GitHub, website.

For contact, Telegram or a phone number: we will arrange a rehearsal and find you on the day.
